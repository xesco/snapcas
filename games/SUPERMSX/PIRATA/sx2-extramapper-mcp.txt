# The title takes the interrupt in the game's routine at 8400h, through JP at
# 0038h in its RAM page 0. From the menu on, the game runs with the interrupts
# off, so the wrapper of 8400h no longer runs. Three routines run at a steady
# rate instead: 9EA6h prints the menu's last lines once in each pass of the
# menu's loop, about 40 times a second; 9FD3h scans the keyboard in the loop
# that shows the credits, about 10 times a second; and B480h runs about 9
# times in each frame of the play, about 47 times a second. So the image gets
# a wrapper for each, at F9A0h, F9B1h, F9C2h and F9D3h.
rescall 8400 9EA6 9FD3 B480

# The four wrappers take 68 bytes, and the 24 bytes at FCC9h, before the tape
# service's part, hold one. F9A0h is in the BIOS's PLAY queues, zeros that the
# game leaves alone.
rescode F9A0

# The snapshot's JP 8400h at 0038h goes to the first wrapper. With the rescall
# line's addresses, the build leaves that jump as it is.
patch page0:0039 00 84 > A0 F9

# The menu's loop at 9E74h calls the wrapper of 9EA6h.
patch page2:1E74 CD A6 9E > CD B1 F9

# The credits' loop at 9E60h calls the wrapper of 9FD3h. The other calls of
# 9FD3h wait for a key in a tight loop and stay as they are.
patch page2:1E60 CD D3 9F > CD C2 F9

# The two calls of B480h in the play call its wrapper.
patch page2:31B2 CD 80 B4 > CD D3 F9
patch page2:3450 CD 80 B4 > CD D3 F9
